## Top Stories

### Ironwood Activates Tuesday — Block 3,428,143

Two days. The biggest Zcash protocol upgrade since Sapling arrives on Tuesday ~12:00 UTC. Ironwood (NU6.3) creates a new shielded pool with formally-verified circuits, permanently sealing the old Orchard pool. The turnstile bounds any theoretical counterfeit ZEC — if it exists, it can never leave. Users on supported wallets (Zodl, Zingo, Noir) don't need to take action — wallets will prompt migration. Node operators must be on Zebra 6.2.2 or Zakura 1.0.4+. Ironwood also introduces quantum recoverability — ebfull: "It wasn't created in two months; it was in progress for more than a year and is riding Ironwood into the Zcash protocol." [CipherScan Ironwood tracker](https://cipherscan.app/ironwood) · [ZODL countdown](https://ironwood.zodl.com/) · [ebfull on quantum recoverability](https://x.com/i/status/2080631357164990592)

### Zebra Ships Four Releases in Ten Days

The Zcash Foundation pushed Zebra 6.1.0, 6.2.0, 6.2.1, and 6.2.2 between July 17 and July 24 — a pre-activation hardening sprint. 6.1.0 patched four security vulnerabilities. 6.2.1 (July 22) added Ironwood-specific mempool defenses, rejecting invalid shielded proofs before expensive verification and disconnecting misbehaving peers. 6.2.2 (July 24) is the recommended build for activation. Meanwhile, Zakura shipped 1.0.2, 1.0.3, and 1.0.4 in the same window — each "20% faster" according to the team. Two independent node teams hardening in parallel. [Zebra releases](https://github.com/ZcashFoundation/zebra/releases) · [Zakura 1.0.4](https://x.com/i/status/2081145910793552128)

### ZCG July 17 Meeting: Five Approved, Nine Declined

The new committee (Brigner, GGuy, Artkor, Zerodartz, Hanh) made their first major batch of decisions. **Approved:** Zcash in the Privacy Stack (#339), Offensive Security (#340, $88K), ZECsy Team Amsterdam (#344, $30K), Zcash Türkiye RLAY HUB (#346), Web3Lagos Onboarding (#349). **Declined in one session:** KeepKey Orchard (#341), Indexer Observatory (#343), Zcash Explained (#345), Zcash Arabia (#347), Light Client Privacy Transport (#348), FROST Multi-Sig SDK (#351), Go Tx Parser (#352), Chadwick Bailey (#353), Turnstile Migration Kit (#357). Nine declines signals a committee willing to hold the bar.

## Privacy Corner: The Turnstile Is the Audit

Tuesday, the Orchard pool seals. Funds can only exit through the turnstile into Ironwood. Here's why that matters beyond the security fix.

The classic critique of shielded pools: "If you can't see inside, how do you know no one printed money?" The answer was always "trust the math." After Orchard, the answer is better: "Watch the exit."

The turnstile is a one-way valve with a hard cap. It knows exactly how much ZEC entered Orchard over its lifetime. It will not allow more than that amount to leave. If counterfeit ZEC exists inside the sealed pool, it's trapped — diluting only the last migrators if the pool is ever fully drained, never the broader supply.

This is supply verification by construction. You don't need to see inside the pool. You watch the boundary. If inflows = 4.2M ZEC over Orchard's lifetime, then outflows through the turnstile are capped at 4.2M ZEC. Period. Any node can verify this. CipherScan tracks it live.

Ironwood's pool goes further — formal verification means the circuits themselves are mathematically proven correct. No amount of clever exploitation can produce more output notes than input notes. The turnstile catches the past. Formal verification prevents the future.

Two layers of supply integrity. Neither requires seeing inside.
